## Problem 10. The Tangent Line as an Application of the Derivative

### Statement

For 

$$f(x) = x^2 - 3x + 1$$

Find the equation of the tangent line at the point with $x = 2$. Give the point of tangency, the value $f'(2)$, the equation of the line, and verify that the point lies on the obtained tangent line.

* **Why this problem:** It combines the derivative and the equation of a line into a complete geometric problem.

---

### Solution

#### 1. Find the point of tangency
Evaluate $f(x)$ at $x = 2$:

$$f(2) = (2)^2 - 3(2) + 1 = 4 - 6 + 1 = -1$$

* **Point of tangency:** $(2, -1)$

#### 2. Compute $f'(x)$ and the value $f'(2)$
Differentiate $f(x)$:

$$f'(x) = 2x - 3$$

Now evaluate $f'(2)$ to find the slope $m$ of the tangent line:

$$f'(2) = 2(2) - 3 = 4 - 3 = 1$$

* **Value of $f'(2)$:** $1$

#### 3. Determine the equation of the tangent line
Using the point-slope form $y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)$ with $(x_0, y_0) = (2, -1)$ and $m = 1$:

$$y - (-1) = 1(x - 2)$$

$$y + 1 = x - 2$$

$$y = x - 3$$

* **Equation of the tangent line:** $y = x - 3$

#### 4. Verify that the point lies on the obtained tangent line
Substitute $x = 2$ into the tangent line equation $y = x - 3$:

$$y = 2 - 3 = -1$$

Since substituting $x = 2$ yields $y = -1$, the point $(2, -1)$ satisfies the equation of the line, confirming that the point of tangency lies on the obtained tangent line[cite: 6].
