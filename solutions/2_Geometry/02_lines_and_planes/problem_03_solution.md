### Exercise 3. Plane Through a Point with a Given Normal Vector

**Statement:**
Find the equation of the plane through $P = (1, -2, 3)$ and perpendicular to $\vec{n} = (2, 1, -1)$. Check the result by substituting the point $P$ and explain the role of the normal vector.

---

### Solution

#### 1. Equation of the Plane
A plane passing through point $P = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ with normal vector $\vec{n} = (a, b, c)$ is given by the equation:

$$
a(x - x_0) + b(y - y_0) + c(z - z_0) = 0
$$

Substituting $P = (1, -2, 3)$ and $\vec{n} = (2, 1, -1)$:

$$
2(x - 1) + 1(y - (-2)) - 1(z - 3) = 0
$$

$$
2(x - 1) + 1(y + 2) - 1(z - 3) = 0
$$

Expanding the terms:

$$
2x - 2 + y + 2 - z + 3 = 0
$$

Rearranging into standard general form:

$$
2x + y - z + 3 = 0
$$

#### 2. Verification
Let's check if point $P = (1, -2, 3)$ satisfies the equation $2x + y - z + 3 = 0$:

$$
2(1) + (-2) - 3 + 3 = 2 - 2 - 3 + 3 = 0 \quad (\text{True})
$$

#### 3. Role of the Normal Vector
The normal vector $\vec{n} = (a, b, c)$ is crucial because:
- **Perpendicularity:** It defines the spatial orientation of the plane by being perpendicular (orthogonal) to every vector lying entirely within the plane.
- **Coefficients in Equation:** Its components directly form the coefficients of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the general linear equation of the plane ($ax + by + cz + d = 0$).
