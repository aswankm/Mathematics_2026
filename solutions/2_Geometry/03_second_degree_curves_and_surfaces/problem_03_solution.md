### Exercise 3. Ellipse

**Statement:**  
For

$$
\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1,
$$

find the semiaxis lengths, the intercepts with the coordinate axes, and make a sketch. Explain along which axis the ellipse is more stretched and how this is visible in the equation.

> **Why this exercise:** teaches how to read the size and orientation of an ellipse without additional conceptual machinery.

---

### Solution

#### 1. Semiaxis Lengths
The canonical equation of an ellipse centered at the origin is:

$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
$$

Comparing terms:

- $a^2 = 25 \implies a = 5$ (semiaxis along the $x$-axis)
- $b^2 = 9 \implies b = 3$ (semiaxis along the $y$-axis)

---

#### 2. Intercepts with the Coordinate Axes
- **$x$-intercepts** (set $y = 0$):  

  $$
  \frac{x^2}{25} + 0 = 1 \implies x^2 = 25 \implies x = \pm 5 \implies (-5, 0) \text{ and } (5, 0)
  $$

- **$y$-intercepts** (set $x = 0$):  

  $$
  0 + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies y^2 = 9 \implies y = \pm 3 \implies (0, -3) \text{ and } (0, 3)
  $$

---

#### 3. Orientation and Stretching Analysis

- **Axis of greater stretching:** The ellipse is **more stretched along the $x$-axis**.
- **How it is visible in the equation:**  
  The denominator under $x^2$ ($25$) is larger than the denominator under $y^2$ ($9$). The larger denominator ($a^2 > b^2$) indicates that the ellipse extends further along the corresponding coordinate axis ($x$-axis), making the horizontal axis the major axis.

---

#### 4. Sketch Description

- **Center:** $(0, 0)$
- **Vertices on Major Axis:** $(-5, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$
- **Vertices on Minor Axis:** $(0, -3)$ and $(0, 3)$
- **Shape:** A smooth, symmetric oval elongated horizontally along the $x$-axis spanning from $x = -5$ to $x = 5$ and vertically from $y = -3$ to $y = 3$.
