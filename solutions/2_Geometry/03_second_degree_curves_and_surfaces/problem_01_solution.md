### Exercise 1. Recognizing Curves from Canonical Form

**Statement:**  
Recognize the curves

$$x^2 + y^2 = 9,$$

$$\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1,$$

$$y^2 = 8x,$$

$$\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1.$$

For each one, identify the most important features and make a sketch. Explain which parts of the equation let you recognize the type of curve.

> **Why this exercise:** builds the basic dictionary: canonical equation $\rightarrow$ type of curve $\rightarrow$ shape and scale.

---

### Solution

#### 1. Analysis of Each Curve

---

##### **Curve 1:** 

$x^2 + y^2 = 9$

* **Recognition:** Both variables are squared, have equal positive coefficients ($1$), and are added together. This is the canonical form of a **Circle** ($x^2 + y^2 = R^2$).
* **Key Features:**
  * **Center:** $(0, 0)$
  * **Radius:** $R = \sqrt{9} = 3$
* **Sketch Description:** A circle centered at the origin passing through $(3,0)$, $(-3,0)$, $(0,3)$, and $(0,-3)$.

---

##### **Curve 2:** 

$\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$

* **Recognition:** Both variables are squared, added together, but have different positive denominators ($\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$). This is an **Ellipse**.
* **Key Features:**
  * **Center:** $(0, 0)$
  * **Semi-major axis:** $a = \sqrt{16} = 4$ along the $x$-axis
  * **Semi-minor axis:** $b = \sqrt{9} = 3$ along the $y$-axis
  * **Foci:** $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{16 - 9} = \sqrt{7}$, located at $(\pm\sqrt{7}, 0)$
* **Sketch Description:** A horizontally elongated ellipse centered at the origin, passing through vertices $(\pm 4, 0)$ and co-vertices $(0, \pm 3)$.

---

##### **Curve 3:** 

$y^2 = 8x$

* **Recognition:** Only one variable ($y$) is squared, while the other ($x$) is linear ($y^2 = 2px$ or $y^2 = 4px$). This is a **Parabola**.
* **Key Features:**
  * **Vertex:** $(0, 0)$
  * **Orientation:** Opens to the right (along the positive $x$-axis because $8 > 0$)
  * **Focal Parameter:** $4p = 8 \implies p = 2$
  * **Focus:** $(p, 0) = (2, 0)$
  * **Directrix:** $x = -p = -2$
* **Sketch Description:** A U-shaped curve with its vertex at the origin opening rightward, passing through $(2, 4)$ and $(2, -4)$.

---

##### **Curve 4:** 

$\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$

* **Recognition:** Both variables are squared, but they are separated by a **minus sign** ($\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$). This is a **Hyperbola**.
* **Key Features:**
  * **Center:** $(0, 0)$
  * **Orientation:** Opens horizontally along the $x$-axis (since $x^2$ is the positive term)
