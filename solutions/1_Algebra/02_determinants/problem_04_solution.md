## Exercise 4. Triangular Matrix

### Compute:
Without expanding the determinant, compute:

$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 1 \\ 0 & -3 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 7 \end{pmatrix}
$$

State the rule for an arbitrary triangular matrix and explain it using this example.

### Solution:

**1. Rule for Triangular Matrices:**
* The determinant of a triangular matrix (upper or lower) is always equal to the **product of its main diagonal entries**.

**2. Calculation for this Example:**
* The main diagonal elements of the given upper triangular matrix are $2$, $-3$, and $7$.
* Multiplying these entries together:

$$
\det = (2) \cdot (-3) \cdot (7) = -42
$$

* **Conclusion:** Without expanding, the determinant is **$-42$** based on the triangular matrix property.
